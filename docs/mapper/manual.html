op1 lfo hero

device report  ·  manual method  ·  ~15 minutes

there is an easier way. if you are on chrome, edge or firefox on a computer, the guided mapper does all of this for you and produces a file to send. this page is the fallback for safari, for anyone who would rather use a midi monitor they already trust, or for anyone the mapper does not work for.

what this is for

lfo hero drives the op-1 field, with tx-6 and tp-7 support in testing. every one of those was mapped by measuring real hardware, because the published midi references turned out to be wrong in at least six places.

to support any other teenage engineering device — op-1 (original), op-z, ep-133, ob-4, po series — we need someone who owns one to record what it actually sends. that is what this page asks you to do. you do not need to know anything about midi.

1 — pick a midi monitor

mac snoize midi monitor free, long-standing, saves the log to a file. use this if you are on safari
windows midi-ox free, the long-standing windows equivalent

plug your device in over usb, open the monitor, and select your device as the input. if you see nothing at all in later steps, check the nothing is showing up section — several te devices transmit nothing until you change a setting on the device itself.

2 — two rules that make or break this

a midi log does not say where a message came from. it records that cc 7 appeared, not that it was track 2's fader. these two rules are the only thing that lets us tell them apart afterwards:

  • do the steps in order, and skip none. if your device has no such control, that is a useful answer — note skipped 6 — no pan and move on. just don't silently leave it out.
  • pause about 3 seconds between steps, touching nothing. the gaps are what chop the log back up into one block per control. without them the log is close to unreadable.

start recording in the monitor, do every step below, then stop and export. one continuous log for the whole run is what we want — please don't export one file per step.

    4 — answer these too

    the log alone doesn't tell us everything. please send answers to these as well:

    • what is the device, and what firmware version is it running?
    • what does the monitor call the port — copy the name and manufacturer exactly as shown, including capitals and punctuation
    • how many tracks did you set above?
    • which steps did you skip, and was it because the control doesn't exist or because you couldn't find it? (these mean very different things to us)
    • did you have to change any setting on the device to make it send midi? this one matters a lot — the tx-6 sends nothing until you set midi control = in and clock src = usb, and we would never have guessed that
    • if your monitor can send midi and you tried it: did the device visibly react? (a fader moving on screen, a value changing.) don't worry if you skipped this

    nothing is showing up

    • try a different usb cable. plenty of usb-c cables are charge-only and carry no data at all.
    • check the device's midi settings. several te devices ship with midi output off, or have mutually exclusive in/out modes where selecting one disables the other.
    • a recorder may only transmit while rolling. the tp-7, for instance, sends nothing at all while the tape is stopped — which looks exactly like broken hardware.
    • if you unplugged and replugged, the monitor may still be listening to the old, dead port. reselect the input, or restart the monitor.
