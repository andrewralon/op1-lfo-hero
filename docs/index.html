supported devices

  • op-1 field — 4 tracks, master fx, tape transport, midi clock both ways
  • tx-6 — 6 tracks plus the master and fx buses (they share the m target), about 17 parameters per channel
  • tp-7 — 6 tracks, no pan. gain 1–3 belong to the three input jacks, not to tracks. record is deliberately not an lfo target

the app picks your device from its midi name, or choose it yourself in settings if you connect through a hub. one device at a time.

connection

  • connect your midi device via usb-c or ble (bluetooth low energy)
  • the layout changes based on the connected device

tempo modes

  • op1 (beat match) / tx6 / tp7 — the device is master. the app's lfos follow its tempo.
  • app (midi sync) — the app is master. it controls basic transport (play/stop, plus back/forward on the op-1 and tp-7, or the −/+ tempo buttons on the tx-6).
    • tempo lfo on the op-1 requires a usb-c connection.
    • note: bluetooth (ble midi) does not send high-resolution tempo changes from the op-1.

toggle the app's tempo mode by tapping the metronome, then change the device to match.

op-1 field setup

  • the op-1's midi sync mode is needed for tape transport from the app.

tx-6 setup

  • set midi control to in. until you do, the tx-6 ignores everything the app sends (and stops sending its own knob and fader messages).
  • set clock SRC to match how you are connected: usb over usb-c, ble over bluetooth. with the wrong one, tempo and play/stop silently do nothing while volume, pan and mute still work.
  • to have the app follow the tx-6 instead, set clock SRC to internal, enable clock out, and tap the metronome until it reads tx6.
  • moving a fader on the tx-6 while an lfo drives that channel fights the lfo — pause the chip first. lfos restart on a play pressed in the app.

tp-7 setup

  • use sync mode for the app to follow the tp-7's tempo. it only streams midi clock while its tape is moving. volume, mute and gain control work in off and sync alike.
  • in ctrl mode the tp-7 acts as a controller for other gear: it will not play back and stops receiving commands from the app.

help wanted — got another te device?

every device lfo hero supports — the op-1 field, tx-6 and tp-7 — was mapped by measuring real hardware, because the published midi references turned out to be wrong in several places. adding the op-1 (original), op-z, ep-133, ob-4 or a po means someone who owns one has to record what it actually sends.

it takes about ten minutes and you don't need to know anything about midi:

  • midi mapper — runs in the browser, walks you through it, produces a file to send. needs chrome, edge or firefox on a computer
  • manual instructions — for safari, or if you'd rather use a midi monitor you already trust
